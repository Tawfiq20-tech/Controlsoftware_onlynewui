# A. EasyCNC — RTS protocol (primary controller)
######################################################################

--- Entry 1 ---
KEY: ALARM: Machine locked (code N). Press Reset to clear.
MEANS: Board reported its stateByte as an alarm state (limit trip, e-stop, or a fault the firmware latched).
FIX: Go to Device tab or the alarm popup and click Clear Alarm. If it re-triggers immediately, a limit switch is still physically pressed or a motor ALM line is stuck low — check wiring before clearing again.
NAV: Alarm popup / Sidebar > Position > Clear Alarm

--- Entry 2 ---
KEY: MOTOR ERROR: Closed-loop position error detected! Position: X=.. Y=.. Z=..
MEANS: The board's closed-loop control detected the commanded position and actual encoder/step position have diverged past tolerance on one or more motors.
FIX: Press Reset to clear, then check the motor's coupling/belt for slipping and the driver's current setting — an undersized current limit causes missed steps under load, which looks identical to this.
NAV: Alarm popup > Reset, or Sidebar > Position > per-motor Reset

--- Entry 3 ---
KEY: HOMING TIMEOUT on <axis> — limit switch not triggered. Check wiring.
MEANS: During the homing cycle, that axis moved its full search distance without the limit switch ever reporting triggered.
FIX: Check the switch's physical wiring (signal + GND), confirm it isn't wired to the wrong axis's input, and jog the axis manually toward the switch to confirm it changes state (Sidebar > Jog, watch the alarm banner).
NAV: Sidebar > Jog (to test) then Home icon in header to retry

--- Entry 4 ---
KEY: Homing cancelled by E-STOP
MEANS: The E-stop was pressed (or the E-stop input tripped) while a homing cycle was in progress.
FIX: Clear the E-stop physically, press Reset in the app, then re-run Home.
NAV: Home icon in top header

--- Entry 5 ---
KEY: *** EMERGENCY STOP — all motion halted ***
MEANS: Software or hardware e-stop fired; RTS abort frame (01 05 00 03 FF) was sent, all motion stopped immediately.
FIX: Confirm the physical e-stop button is released, then use the E-STOP clear button in the job bar or alarm popup before resuming.
NAV: JobControlBar E-STOP button / alarm popup

--- Entry 6 ---
KEY: E-STOP clear sent...
MEANS: Host sent the command to clear a latched e-stop condition on the board.
FIX: Wait for the board to report a ready/idle state; if it stays in alarm, the physical e-stop switch itself may still be pressed or wired normally-closed and broken open.

--- Entry 7 ---
KEY: Limit error cleared — machine ready
MEANS: A previously-tripped limit switch alarm has been successfully cleared and the board reports ready.
FIX: No action needed — this is a confirmation, not an error. If it re-alarms right after jogging, the switch may be mis-adjusted (triggering too early in travel).

--- Entry 8 ---
KEY: Motor errors: <list> / All motor errors cleared
MEANS: One or more of X/Y1/Y2/Z motors reported an ALM (fault) line; the second variant is the all-clear confirmation after Reset.
FIX: Reset the specific motor(s) named. If the same motor keeps faulting, check its driver's fault output wiring and that its current/microstep DIP switches match firmware expectations.
NAV: Sidebar > Position, per-motor Reset button

--- Entry 9 ---
KEY: Config: <parameter> = <value>
MEANS: Board acknowledged a firmware configuration parameter was read or set (e.g. steps/mm, max travel).
FIX: Informational only — confirm the value matches what you expect for that axis; a wrong value here (like max_travel=0) is a known cause of ECSS pre-flight false positives.
NAV: Settings > CNC

--- Entry 10 ---
KEY: Connection stale — no response for Nms / Connection appears dead — 10+ missed polls
MEANS: The host has been polling the board's status but hasn't received a reply for many consecutive polls — the USB/serial link has effectively gone silent.
FIX: Check the USB cable and port; reconnect from the Device tab. If it happens repeatedly, try a different USB port/cable or a shorter, better-shielded cable (common on machines near a VFD or spindle).
NAV: Device tab > Disconnect then Connect

--- Entry 11 ---
KEY: Bad frame end byte at length N
MEANS: A received RTS frame from the board didn't end with the expected terminator byte — usually a corrupted/partial USB packet.
FIX: Usually self-heals on the next frame. If it repeats constantly, it points to electrical noise on the USB line (route it away from stepper/spindle wiring) or a marginal USB cable.

--- Entry 12 ---
KEY: Short status frame: N bytes
MEANS: A status frame from the board arrived shorter than the protocol expects — likely a dropped/truncated USB packet.
FIX: Same cause/fix as 'Bad frame end byte' — check cable/EMI. Isolated occurrences are harmless; the next poll recovers.

--- Entry 13 ---
KEY: JSON parse error: <message>
MEANS: A frame that was expected to carry a JSON payload from the board failed to parse.
FIX: Usually transient corruption on the wire (see cable/EMI notes above). If constant, confirm firmware and host protocol versions match.

--- Entry 14 ---
KEY: ack-timeout on line N after 500ms
MEANS: The host sent a G-code line and never got the expected 'ok'/ack from the board within the timeout window.
FIX: One-off: harmless, the line gets retried/backed off automatically. Frequent: the board's planner may be overloaded or the USB link is dropping bytes — try a lower feed rate or check the cable.

--- Entry 15 ---
KEY: Unknown command: "<cmd>"
MEANS: The host sent a command string the firmware doesn't recognise (usually only during development or after a frontend/firmware version mismatch).
FIX: Confirm frontend and firmware build versions are the pair they were tested together on; report to the dev team if it appears during normal use.

--- Entry 16 ---
KEY: Invalid home axis: <axis>
MEANS: The Home command was issued with an axis identifier the firmware doesn't recognise (should only be X/Y/Z/ALL).
FIX: This indicates a UI/host bug, not a hardware fault — retry Home from the standard button rather than a custom script.

--- Entry 17 ---
KEY: No homing vector for axis <axis> — using fallback
MEANS: The firmware's per-axis homing direction/vector table has no entry for this axis; it fell back to a default.
FIX: Check Settings > CNC machine profile for that axis's homing direction is actually configured; an unset direction can home into the wrong end of travel.
NAV: Settings > CNC

--- Entry 18 ---
KEY: motion complete TIMEOUT (Nms) during <phase> — proceeding
MEANS: During homing, the board didn't confirm 'motion complete' for a phase (seek/backoff/precise) within the timeout, so the host moved on anyway.
FIX: Usually harmless if homing still finishes correctly. If the axis ends up in the wrong place, it points to a motion-complete detection issue at that travel speed — reduce homing feed rate in Settings > CNC.

--- Entry 19 ---
KEY: bulk-push: N retries on line M — giving up, advancing to avoid stall
MEANS: During a fast bulk G-code push, one line failed to get acknowledged after repeated retries; the host skipped it to avoid stalling the whole job.
FIX: That line was NOT executed — stop the job and inspect the file around that line number; this usually means the board briefly missed a packet under heavy load.

--- Entry 20 ---
KEY: bulk-push ack-timeout on line N after 100ms — advancing
MEANS: Same as above but a single miss, not exhausted retries — one bulk-push line's ack didn't arrive in time so the host moved on.
FIX: Usually self-healing; watch for the line count in the progress bar matching the file's total at the end of the job.

--- Entry 21 ---
KEY: Resuming stream after board exited Hold
MEANS: The board's own Hold state (feed-hold) cleared and the host is now sending G-code again automatically.
FIX: Informational — confirms pause/resume worked correctly.

--- Entry 22 ---
KEY: START pressed but no G-code is loaded on the controller — re-upload the file.
MEANS: Start was pressed but the backend has no G-code content associated with the current session (can happen after a reconnect).
FIX: Reload the file from the Sidebar's File Management section, then press Start again.
NAV: Sidebar > File Management > Browse for G-Code File

--- Entry 23 ---
KEY: START pressed but a job is already running.
MEANS: Start was pressed while the backend already considers a job active — a double-click or stale UI state.
FIX: No action needed if the job is actually running; if the UI looks stuck, refresh the page/reconnect.

--- Entry 24 ---
KEY: _startJob called but no G-code loaded — START is a no-op. Did file:load fire?
MEANS: Backend-side log confirming the same no-file condition as above, useful for debugging a silent Start.
FIX: Same fix — reload the file before starting.

# B. EasyCNC — RSP protocol
######################################################################

--- Entry 25 ---
KEY: Recovered a job that was running before the last restart. Press START to resume from line N...
MEANS: On file load, the backend found a saved resume checkpoint (data/rsp_resume_state.json) matching this file's hash from before a backend restart or crash.
FIX: If you want to actually resume, press Start — it will skip to that line. If you want to start over, load a fresh copy of the file or edit+resave it (changes the hash) before pressing Start.
NAV: Sidebar > File Management, then JobControlBar Start

--- Entry 26 ---
KEY: Resuming from line N (where it was stopped).
MEANS: Start was pressed after a deliberate Stop on the same loaded file; RSP protocol is resuming from the exact next-unexecuted line rather than restarting at line 1.
FIX: Confirm the bit is positioned correctly for that line before starting — the resume does not automatically re-home or re-probe.

--- Entry 27 ---
KEY: Stopped at line N. Press START to resume from here, or load a new file to restart.
MEANS: Stop was pressed; the RSP job engine checkpointed the exact resume point.
FIX: Informational. Press Start to continue from N, or load a different file to discard the checkpoint.

--- Entry 28 ---
KEY: link down mid-job — waiting up to 15s for it to recover before aborting
MEANS: The RSP link's heartbeat has gone silent for >3s during an active job; the host is giving the USB link 15 seconds to self-heal before treating it as a real failure.
FIX: Usually resolves on its own if it was a transient USB blip. If it consistently aborts after 15s, check the cable/port — the link is not recovering.

--- Entry 29 ---
KEY: link recovered after N.Ns, resuming job
MEANS: The RSP link came back within the 15-second grace window and the job continued automatically.
FIX: No action needed — confirms the self-heal worked.

--- Entry 30 ---
KEY: execution stalled at N/M for Ns — giving up
MEANS: No forward execution progress (no EV_EXECUTED, no EV_JOB_DONE) was seen for 90 continuous seconds; the host gave up on the job.
FIX: Check the board is powered and not wedged (try a status query/reset). If moves are just legitimately slow (deep cuts, small stepovers), this threshold was widened once already (2026-08-27) — report if it's still firing on real, alive jobs.

--- Entry 31 ---
KEY: job stream interrupted: <exception message>
MEANS: The RSP job's sender loop hit an unrecoverable error (usually LinkLost after the 15s grace window expired).
FIX: Reconnect from the Device tab; if a resume checkpoint exists, the next file load will offer to resume from where it stopped.
NAV: Device tab

--- Entry 32 ---
KEY: job end failed: <message>
MEANS: The final OP_JOB_END handshake with the board didn't complete cleanly after the job's last line executed.
FIX: The job likely still finished — check the part. This is a protocol-teardown message, not evidence the job itself failed.

# B. EasyCNC — RSP protocol status codes (device-reported)
######################################################################

--- Entry 33 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_INTERNAL (0x01)
MEANS: Device-side internal firmware error.
FIX: Power-cycle the board; if it recurs, capture the exact command sequence and report it — this indicates a firmware bug, not a wiring issue.

--- Entry 34 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_BUSY (0x02)
MEANS: Device is busy and can't accept the command right now.
FIX: Wait and retry; the host protocol normally retries this automatically. Persistent busy usually means the planner is backed up — reduce feed or check for a stall.

--- Entry 35 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_STATE (0x03)
MEANS: Command isn't allowed in the board's current machine state (e.g. trying to jog while a job is running).
FIX: Bring the machine to the right state first (stop the job, clear the alarm) then retry the command.

--- Entry 36 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_JOB (0x04)
MEANS: job_id mismatch, or the command referenced a job that isn't currently active on the device.
FIX: Usually a stale/leftover message from a previous job — safe to ignore if a new job is running correctly. If it blocks a real job, reload the file and restart.

--- Entry 37 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_SEQ_GAP (0x05)
MEANS: The device detected an out-of-window sequence number; frames were lost or arrived out of order.
FIX: The host automatically resends from the gap. Frequent occurrences point to a noisy/marginal USB link.

--- Entry 38 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_CRC (0x06)
MEANS: A received frame failed its CRC32 check — corrupted in transit.
FIX: Same cause as SEQ_GAP — check USB cable routing away from motor/spindle wiring; the frame gets retransmitted automatically.

--- Entry 39 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_BUFFER (0x07)
MEANS: The device's motion planner buffer is full (flow-control backpressure, not a fault).
FIX: Normal under high feed rates — the host's windowed pipelining backs off and retries. Not an error to act on unless the job stalls entirely.

--- Entry 40 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_CMD (0x08)
MEANS: Unknown command or malformed payload sent to the device.
FIX: Indicates a host/firmware version mismatch — confirm both are the matched pair; report if seen during normal operation.

--- Entry 41 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_ESTOP (0x09)
MEANS: E-stop is latched on the device; it refuses to execute anything until cleared.
FIX: Physically release the e-stop, then send the clear/reset command from the app.

--- Entry 42 ---
KEY: ST_ERR_FAULT (0x0A)
MEANS: An axis fault (ALM line asserted) is preventing the command.
FIX: Check that axis's driver fault output and wiring; Reset the specific motor from Sidebar > Position.

# C. EasyCNC — ECSS safety system messages
######################################################################

--- Entry 43 ---
KEY: [ECSS] Pre-flight BLOCKED — N issue(s). probableCorruptWCS=true/false
MEANS: ECSS Module 1 scanned the loaded G-code against machine travel bounds before Start and found it would go out of bounds.
FIX: Read the specific issue lines logged right after this one — they name the axis/coordinate that's out of range. If probableCorruptWCS is true, your work coordinate offset is likely wrong, not the file — check Position tab for a wildly off G54 offset before blaming the file.
NAV: Sidebar > Position (check WCS offsets)

--- Entry 44 ---
KEY: [ECSS] Start BLOCKED by pre-flight: <reasons>
MEANS: Same as above — Start/Resume was refused because the pre-flight envelope check failed.
FIX: Fix the underlying cause (bad WCS offset or a genuinely out-of-bounds file) rather than repeatedly retrying Start.

--- Entry 45 ---
KEY: [ECSS] Pre-flight BLOCK overridden by user — proceeding with <cmd>. (Module 3 Z watchdog still active.)
MEANS: A one-shot manual override was used to bypass the pre-flight block for this Start.
FIX: Only use this for a deliberate diagnostic move you understand is safe — the override consumes itself after one Start and re-arms the block for the next one.

--- Entry 46 ---
KEY: [ECSS] WCS health: <axis>=<value> (threshold ±50 mm).
MEANS: ECSS Module 2 flagged a work coordinate offset (G54 etc.) more than 50mm from zero — a common signature of NVRAM/EEPROM corruption on the board.
FIX: Re-zero that axis at a known-good physical reference point (Sidebar > Position > Zero) before running anything — a corrupted offset can send the machine somewhere unexpected on the very first move.
NAV: Sidebar > Position

--- Entry 47 ---
KEY: [ECSS] Z RUNAWAY fired: drop=N mm in M ms, mposZ=Z
MEANS: ECSS Module 3 detected the Z axis dropping further/faster than physically expected for a controlled move and fired an emergency abort (0x03).
FIX: Stop and physically inspect Z before doing anything else — this is the watchdog that exists specifically to catch a runaway plunge. Note: as of the last audit this module is gated behind an env var that's unset by default, so confirm it's actually enabled in your deployment before relying on it.

--- Entry 48 ---
KEY: [Engine] command '<cmd>' rejected: no active controller
MEANS: A command was sent to the backend but no controller is currently connected to a board.
FIX: Go to Device tab and connect before issuing any commands.
NAV: Device tab

--- Entry 49 ---
KEY: [Engine] file:load rejected: no active controller / missing content
MEANS: A G-code file load was attempted with no board connected, or the uploaded content was empty.
FIX: Connect first, and confirm the file you're dragging in actually has content (0-byte files are rejected).

--- Entry 50 ---
KEY: Failed to open <port>: <message>
MEANS: The backend tried to open the selected serial/USB port and the OS refused.
FIX: See Section D (serial/connection errors) below for the specific OS-level reason this usually carries.

# D. EasyCNC — Probing
######################################################################

--- Entry 51 ---
KEY: Probing already in progress
MEANS: A new probe cycle was started while one was already running.
FIX: Wait for the current probe step to finish (or cancel it in the wizard) before starting another.

--- Entry 52 ---
KEY: No active controller
MEANS: Probe was started with no board connected.
FIX: Connect on the Device tab first.

--- Entry 53 ---
KEY: Probing aborted
MEANS: The user cancelled the probe cycle mid-run (Abort button in the probe wizard).
FIX: Restart the probe wizard from Step 1 if you still need to set your zero.

--- Entry 54 ---
KEY: X probe did not contact / Y probe did not contact / <axis> probe did not contact
MEANS: During an XYZ corner-block probe, that axis's touch move completed its full travel without the probe circuit ever triggering.
FIX: Check the probe clip is actually touching bare metal (not paint/coating) on both the bit and the block, and that the bit is positioned close enough to the block before starting that step.
NAV: Probe wizard Step 2 — rejog closer, then retry Step 3

--- Entry 55 ---
KEY: Strategy "<name>" has no RSP-native routine yet
MEANS: The selected probing strategy isn't implemented for the RSP protocol path yet.
FIX: Use the Z Probe or XYZ Probe strategies from the standard wizard — this only affects less-common/experimental probe routines.

--- Entry 56 ---
KEY: Controller ack timeout
MEANS: The probe move was sent but the board never acknowledged it in time.
FIX: Check the connection (Device tab); if the link is fine, retry the probe step once.

--- Entry 57 ---
KEY: Controller has no RSP-native move primitive
MEANS: An internal mismatch between the probing code and the active controller type.
FIX: Should not occur in normal use — if seen, note which controller protocol (RTS vs RSP) was active and report it.

# B. EasyCNC — RSP protocol
######################################################################

--- Entry 58 ---
KEY: [RSP] EV_FAULT axis=<axis> code=<code>
MEANS: The RSP device sent an explicit axis-fault event for a specific axis and fault code.
FIX: Note the axis and code, Reset that motor from Sidebar > Position, and check its driver's fault output wiring if it repeats.

--- Entry 59 ---
KEY: [RSP] EV_ESTOP received
MEANS: The RSP device reported its e-stop input has been triggered.
FIX: Physically release the e-stop, then clear it from the app before resuming.

--- Entry 60 ---
KEY: [RSP] EV_COMM_LOST received (device entered safe-stop)
MEANS: The device itself detected a communications loss and dropped into its own safe-stop state independent of the host's link-loss handling.
FIX: Reconnect from the Device tab; check the resume-checkpoint prompt on next file load if a job was running.

--- Entry 61 ---
KEY: [RSP] resume-state load failed: <message>
MEANS: The backend couldn't read data/rsp_resume_state.json on startup (missing, corrupted, or a permissions issue).
FIX: Not fatal — it just means no resume checkpoint is offered on next file load. If this should have a checkpoint, check file permissions on the backend's data directory.

--- Entry 62 ---
KEY: [RSP] resume-state write failed: <message>
MEANS: The backend couldn't write the resume checkpoint file when a job stopped — a later crash/restart won't be able to offer a resume.
FIX: Check the backend process has write permission to its data directory and that the disk isn't full.

--- Entry 63 ---
KEY: [RSP] command "<cmd>" not supported by RSP firmware — ignored
MEANS: A command was sent that this firmware build's RSP dialect doesn't implement.
FIX: Confirm you're on a firmware build that matches what the frontend expects; not all commands exist on every RSP firmware revision.

--- Entry 64 ---
KEY: [RSP] op 0x<hex> not sent — link down
MEANS: An operation couldn't be transmitted because the RSP link is currently down.
FIX: Reconnect; the specific command was dropped, not queued, so retry it manually once the link is back.

--- Entry 65 ---
KEY: [RSP] probe failed: <message>
MEANS: The RSP-native probe routine threw an error mid-cycle.
FIX: Check probe wiring/continuity and retry from the probe wizard's current step.

--- Entry 66 ---
KEY: [RSP] Controller already bound, unbinding first
MEANS: A new connection attempt found an existing RSP binding still active and cleaned it up automatically before proceeding.
FIX: Informational — no action needed unless connects consistently take two tries.

--- Entry 67 ---
KEY: [RSP] bind() called with no connection
MEANS: Internal sequencing issue — the controller tried to bind before a transport connection existed.
FIX: Disconnect fully and reconnect from the Device tab; report if this repeats.

KEY: [RTS] <axis>: switch triggered — next axis...
MEANS: During the homing sequence, this axis's limit switch was detected and homing is moving on to the next axis in the Z→X→Y order.
FIX: Informational progress message — no action needed unless the sequence stalls after this.

KEY: [RTS] <axis> axis homed — position zeroed / Homing complete — all axes zeroed
MEANS: Confirms that axis (or all axes) successfully completed homing and had its position set to zero at the home switch.

MEANS: Progress message while a per-motor clear-fault sequence is running for that specific motor.

# E. Serial / USB connection (OS-level)
######################################################################

--- Entry 72 ---
KEY: Error: Opening COMx: Access is denied (Windows)
MEANS: Another program already has the COM port open, or Windows hasn't released it from a previous session.
FIX: Close any other terminal/Arduino IDE/serial monitor using that port, unplug/replug the USB cable, then reconnect from the Device tab.
NAV: Device tab

--- Entry 73 ---
KEY: Error: No such file or directory, cannot open /dev/ttyUSB0 (Linux)
MEANS: The port path no longer exists — the board was unplugged, or it enumerated under a different /dev/ttyUSBx or /dev/ttyACMx number.
FIX: Re-plug the USB cable, refresh the port dropdown on the Device tab, and pick the currently-listed port.
NAV: Device tab > Select a device...

--- Entry 74 ---
KEY: Error: Permission denied, cannot open /dev/ttyUSB0 (Linux)
MEANS: Your user account isn't in the group that owns the serial device (usually 'dialout' on Debian/Ubuntu).
FIX: Add your user to the dialout group (sudo usermod -a -G dialout $USER) and log out/in, or run with sudo as a quick test only.

--- Entry 75 ---
KEY: Error: Resource temporarily unavailable / EBUSY
MEANS: The port is already open by another process.
FIX: Close other serial tools (Arduino Serial Monitor, PuTTY, another instance of this app) that might be holding the port.

--- Entry 76 ---
KEY: Port simply disappears from the dropdown mid-session
MEANS: The USB cable was unplugged, the board reset/browned-out, or a USB hub lost power.
FIX: Check the physical cable and any USB hub power; try a direct connection to the PC without a hub.

--- Entry 77 ---
KEY: No response after Connect (status stays 'Connecting...')
MEANS: Wrong baud rate selected, or the wrong device was picked from the port list (e.g. a Bluetooth virtual COM port instead of the CNC board).
FIX: Confirm baud rate matches firmware (default 115200, some boards use 230400) and that you picked the CNC board's actual port, not an unrelated device.
NAV: Device tab > Baud Rate

--- Entry 78 ---
KEY: Connection Error status pill
MEANS: A general catch-all shown when the port opened but the handshake/init sequence with the firmware failed.
FIX: Try Disconnect then Connect again; if it persists, power-cycle the board.

--- Entry 79 ---
KEY: USB device re-enumerates under a new port number after every reset
MEANS: Normal behavior on some OS/driver combos (especially after a board reset via DTR toggle) — not a fault by itself.
FIX: Just re-select the new port number in the dropdown; consider a machine profile pinned by serial number if this is disruptive.

--- Entry 80 ---
KEY: Driver not installed / device shows as 'Unknown Device' in Device Manager (Windows)
MEANS: The board's USB-to-serial chip (CH340, CP2102, or STM32 CDC) doesn't have a driver installed on this PC.
FIX: Install the matching USB driver for your board's chip before it will appear in the port dropdown.

--- Entry 81 ---
KEY: App shows Connected but position never updates
MEANS: The port opened successfully but the board isn't actually sending status frames — often a baud mismatch or a firmware that isn't fully booted.
FIX: Power-cycle the board, reconnect, and confirm baud rate; check the boot banner if you have a raw terminal available.

--- Entry 82 ---
KEY: Multiple ports listed, unsure which is the CNC board
MEANS: Normal on a PC with other serial devices (Arduino, Bluetooth, modem chipsets) attached.
FIX: Unplug the CNC board's USB cable, note which entry disappears from the dropdown, replug and pick that one.

--- Entry 83 ---
KEY: Intermittent disconnects only under spindle load
MEANS: Electrical noise from the spindle/VFD coupling into the USB cable or a shared ground loop.
FIX: Route the USB cable away from spindle/VFD power wiring, use a ferrite choke on the USB cable, and confirm the VFD's shielding/grounding is correct.

--- Entry 84 ---
KEY: Connection works on one USB port but not another on the same PC
MEANS: Some USB 3.0 ports have known compatibility issues with certain USB-serial chipsets, or a hub-vs-direct difference.
FIX: Prefer a USB 2.0 port or a direct connection (no hub) if you see this.

--- Entry 85 ---
KEY: Baud rate mismatch symptom: garbage/binary characters in a raw terminal
MEANS: Terminal and board are talking at different baud rates.
FIX: Match the app's baud rate setting to the firmware's configured baud (115200 default, 230400 on some builds).
NAV: Device tab > Baud Rate

# F. Standard GRBL error codes (generic/GRBL-fallback path)
######################################################################

--- Entry 86 ---
KEY: error:1
MEANS: G-code words consist of a letter and a value. Letter was not found.
FIX: Check the line for a stray letter with no following number, or a corrupted line from a bad export — re-slice the file.

--- Entry 87 ---
KEY: error:2
MEANS: Numeric value format is not valid or missing an expected value.
FIX: A number is malformed (e.g. 'X.' with nothing after the decimal) — fix or re-export the G-code.

--- Entry 88 ---
KEY: error:3
MEANS: Grbl '$' system command was not recognized or supported.
FIX: Check the $-command syntax; you may be sending a setting command the firmware build doesn't support.

--- Entry 89 ---
KEY: error:4
MEANS: Negative value received for an expected positive value.
FIX: A setting or parameter that must be positive (like step pulse time) was sent negative — check the value being written.

--- Entry 90 ---
KEY: error:5
MEANS: Homing cycle is not enabled via settings.
FIX: Enable homing ($22=1) in firmware settings before using $H.

--- Entry 91 ---
KEY: error:6
MEANS: Minimum step pulse time must be greater than 3usec.
FIX: Increase the $0 step pulse time setting — a value under 3us is rejected.

--- Entry 92 ---
KEY: error:7
MEANS: EEPROM read failed. Reset and restored to default values.
FIX: Firmware settings storage was corrupted and reset to defaults — re-enter your machine's calibrated settings.

--- Entry 93 ---
KEY: error:8
MEANS: Grbl '$' command cannot be used unless Grbl is IDLE.
FIX: Stop or finish the current job before changing settings.

--- Entry 94 ---
KEY: error:9
MEANS: G-code locked out during alarm or jog state.
FIX: Clear the alarm (or wait for the jog to finish) before sending G-code.

--- Entry 95 ---
KEY: error:10
MEANS: Soft limits cannot be enabled without homing also enabled.
FIX: Enable homing ($22=1) before enabling soft limits ($20=1).

--- Entry 96 ---
KEY: error:11
MEANS: Max characters per line exceeded.
FIX: That G-code line is too long — split it or re-export from your CAM software with shorter lines.

--- Entry 97 ---
KEY: error:12
MEANS: '$' setting value exceeds the maximum step rate supported.
FIX: Lower the steps/mm or max-rate setting that's pushing the step rate too high for the firmware to generate.

--- Entry 98 ---
KEY: error:13
MEANS: Safety door detected as opened.
FIX: Close the safety door switch/interlock before resuming.

--- Entry 99 ---
KEY: error:14
MEANS: Build info or startup line exceeded EEPROM line length limit.
FIX: Shorten the custom startup line stored in firmware settings.

--- Entry 100 ---
KEY: error:15
MEANS: Jog target exceeds machine travel. Command ignored.
FIX: Reduce the jog distance/step size — you're commanding past the configured travel limits.

--- Entry 101 ---
KEY: error:16
MEANS: Jog command with no '=' or contains prohibited g-code.
FIX: Malformed jog command from the UI/host — should not occur in normal use; report if seen.

--- Entry 102 ---
KEY: error:17
MEANS: Laser mode requires PWM output.
FIX: Only relevant if laser mode ($32=1) is enabled without a PWM-capable spindle output configured.

--- Entry 103 ---
KEY: error:20
MEANS: Unsupported or invalid g-code command found in block.
FIX: That G-code command isn't supported by this firmware — check for CAM-specific codes not meant for this controller.

--- Entry 104 ---
KEY: error:21
MEANS: More than one g-code command from same modal group found in block.
FIX: Two conflicting commands (e.g. two motion modes) are on the same line — fix the G-code source.

--- Entry 105 ---
KEY: error:22
MEANS: Feed rate has not yet been set or is undefined.
FIX: A G1/G2/G3 move was commanded before any F (feed rate) word was ever set — add an F word earlier in the file.

--- Entry 106 ---
KEY: error:23
MEANS: G-code command in block requires an integer value.
FIX: A parameter that must be a whole number (like a tool number) was given a decimal — fix the line.

--- Entry 107 ---
KEY: error:24
MEANS: Two G-code commands that both require XYZ axis words were detected in the block.
FIX: Conflicting motion commands on one line — split them onto separate lines.

--- Entry 108 ---
KEY: error:25
MEANS: A G-code word was repeated in the block.
FIX: The same letter (e.g. two X words) appears twice on one line — fix the source file.

--- Entry 109 ---
KEY: error:26
MEANS: A G-code command requires XYZ axis words in the block, but none were detected.
FIX: A move command has no axis coordinates at all — check for a truncated/corrupted line.

--- Entry 110 ---
KEY: error:27
MEANS: N line number value is not within the valid range of 1 - 9,999,999.
FIX: Line numbering in the file is out of GRBL's supported range — most CAM posts don't include N numbers; remove them if present.

--- Entry 111 ---
KEY: error:28
MEANS: A G-code command is missing a required P or L value word.
FIX: A command like G4 (dwell) needs a P value — check the line for a missing parameter.

--- Entry 112 ---
KEY: error:29
MEANS: Work coordinate systems G59.1, G59.2, G59.3 are not supported.
FIX: Only G54-G59 (not G59.1-3) are supported — change the WCS selection in your CAM post.

--- Entry 113 ---
KEY: error:30
MEANS: G53 requires an active G0 or G1 motion mode.
FIX: G53 (machine-coordinate move) needs a linear motion mode active first — add a G0/G1 before it.

--- Entry 114 ---
KEY: error:31
MEANS: Unused axis words in the block with G80 motion mode cancel active.
FIX: Leftover axis words on a canned-cycle-cancel line — clean up the G-code.

--- Entry 115 ---
KEY: error:32
MEANS: G2/G3 arc commanded but no XYZ axis words in the selected plane.
FIX: An arc move needs at least one axis word in the active plane (G17/18/19) — check the line.

--- Entry 116 ---
KEY: error:33
MEANS: The motion command has an invalid target.
FIX: Generated by G2/G3/G38.2 when the target point is unreachable/invalid — check for a corrupted toolpath.

--- Entry 117 ---
KEY: error:34
MEANS: Arc traced with radius definition had a math error computing geometry.
FIX: Break the arc into smaller semi-circle/quadrant segments, or re-export using IJK offsets instead of R.

--- Entry 118 ---
KEY: error:35
MEANS: Arc traced with offset definition is missing the IJK offset word.
FIX: An I/J/K-defined arc is missing its offset word for the active plane — check the CAM post configuration.

--- Entry 119 ---
KEY: error:36
MEANS: Unused, leftover G-code words that aren't used by any command in the block.
FIX: Extra words on the line that don't belong to any active command — clean up the G-code source.

--- Entry 120 ---
KEY: error:37
MEANS: G43.1 dynamic tool length offset cannot apply to an axis other than its configured axis.
FIX: Tool length offset is being applied to the wrong axis — check the post-processor's TLO axis setting.

--- Entry 121 ---
KEY: error:38
MEANS: Tool number greater than max supported value.
FIX: The T (tool) number in the file exceeds what the firmware allows — check your tool numbering.


######################################################################
# G. Standard GRBL ALARM codes (generic/GRBL-fallback path)
######################################################################

--- Entry 122 ---
KEY: ALARM:1
MEANS: Hard limit triggered. Machine position is likely lost due to a sudden halt.
FIX: Re-home the machine before doing anything else — position after a hard-limit trip cannot be trusted.

--- Entry 123 ---
KEY: ALARM:2
MEANS: G-code motion target exceeds machine travel. Position safely retained.
FIX: Safe to unlock ($X) — the move was rejected before it happened. Fix the offending line/offset before retrying.

--- Entry 124 ---
KEY: ALARM:3
MEANS: Reset while in motion. Lost steps are likely.
FIX: Re-home the machine — position accuracy cannot be guaranteed after an in-motion reset.

--- Entry 125 ---
KEY: ALARM:4
MEANS: Probe fail — probe not in the expected initial state before the probe cycle started.
FIX: For G38.2/G38.3 the probe must start untriggered; for G38.4/G38.5 it must start triggered. Check the probe isn't already touching before you start.

--- Entry 126 ---
KEY: ALARM:5
MEANS: Probe fail — probe did not contact the workpiece within programmed travel.
FIX: Reposition closer to the surface/touch plate and retry, or check the probe wiring/continuity.

--- Entry 127 ---
KEY: ALARM:6
MEANS: Homing fail — active homing cycle was reset.
FIX: Re-run the homing cycle from a clear state; don't reset mid-home.

--- Entry 128 ---
KEY: ALARM:7
MEANS: Homing fail — safety door opened during the homing cycle.
FIX: Close the door/interlock and re-run homing.

--- Entry 129 ---
KEY: ALARM:8
MEANS: Homing fail — pull-off travel failed to clear the limit switch.
FIX: Increase the pull-off distance setting, or check the switch for mechanical binding.

--- Entry 130 ---
KEY: ALARM:9
MEANS: Homing fail — could not find the limit switch within the search distance.
FIX: Check switch wiring and that the axis actually travels toward the switch during homing (direction setting).

--- Entry 131 ---
KEY: ALARM:10
MEANS: Homing fail — second dual-axis limit switch failed to trigger within travel distance.
FIX: On a dual-motor axis (e.g. Y1/Y2), check the second switch's wiring — only relevant on Grbl-Mega/dual-axis builds.

# H. Job / file loading
######################################################################

--- Entry 132 ---
KEY: File rejected — unsupported extension
MEANS: The dragged/browsed file isn't one of the accepted types.
FIX: Accepted extensions are .nc .gcode .txt .ngc .cnc .tap — re-export from your CAM software with one of these.

--- Entry 133 ---
KEY: File loads but line count shows 0
MEANS: The file was empty, or every line was a comment and got filtered out during parsing.
FIX: Re-check the export from your CAM software — confirm it actually contains motion commands, not just headers/comments.

--- Entry 134 ---
KEY: "Reload Current" needed after an edit
MEANS: You edited the G-code file externally after loading it into the app; the app is still holding the old copy.
FIX: Click Reload Current in File Management to re-read the file from disk.
NAV: Sidebar > File Management > Reload Current

--- Entry 135 ---
KEY: Job progress bar stuck at 0% after Start
MEANS: The job was accepted but no lines are executing — often a stale controller reference after a reconnect.
FIX: Stop, reload the file fresh, and Start again; if it persists, disconnect/reconnect on the Device tab first.

--- Entry 136 ---
KEY: Job finishes early / fewer lines executed than the file contains
MEANS: Lines were skipped due to ack-timeouts/retries being exhausted (see bulk-push retry messages in Section A), or the file had trailing comment-only lines that don't count as executable.
FIX: Compare the reported executed-line count to the file's total; check the console log around any skipped-line warnings.

--- Entry 137 ---
KEY: Can't load a new file — 'a job is already running'
MEANS: A previous job wasn't fully stopped before trying to load a new one.
FIX: Press Stop first, confirm the job bar shows idle, then load the new file.


######################################################################
# I. General hardware troubleshooting (root causes behind the alarms above)
######################################################################

--- Entry 138 ---
KEY: Machine homes to the wrong end of an axis
MEANS: Homing direction setting for that axis doesn't match which end the limit switch is physically mounted on.
FIX: Flip the homing direction bit for that axis in Settings > CNC / firmware config, or physically relocate the switch.

--- Entry 139 ---
KEY: One axis moves the opposite direction from the jog button pressed
MEANS: That axis's direction (DIR) signal or motor wiring is inverted relative to firmware convention.
FIX: Invert the direction-polarity setting for that axis in firmware config rather than re-wiring the motor.

--- Entry 140 ---
KEY: Dual-motor axis (Y1/Y2) racks out of square over time
MEANS: One of the two Y motors is losing steps under load (current too low) or the belts have different tension.
FIX: Match belt tension on both sides and confirm both Y drivers have identical current settings; re-square using the machine's squaring procedure after fixing.

--- Entry 141 ---
KEY: Spindle doesn't turn on with M3
MEANS: VFD/relay wiring to the spindle control output isn't connected, or the spindle enable output pin isn't configured in firmware.
FIX: Verify continuity from the control board's spindle output to the VFD's run/enable input; check Sidebar > Controls > Spindle shows the command was actually sent.

--- Entry 142 ---
KEY: Coolant relay doesn't switch with M7/M8
MEANS: Coolant output pin wiring or relay board power is disconnected.
FIX: Check the relay board has power and the control signal reaches it; test the relay manually with a multimeter.

--- Entry 143 ---
KEY: Limit switch triggers randomly with nothing touching it
MEANS: Electrical noise (EMI) on an unshielded switch wire, especially near stepper/spindle cabling, or a floating input with no pull-up/pull-down.
FIX: Use shielded cable for switch wiring, route away from motor power leads, and confirm firmware enables an internal pull-up/pull-down on that input.

--- Entry 144 ---
KEY: Probe gives inconsistent Z-zero results between runs
MEANS: Probe tip contact surface is dirty/oxidized, or the touch plate isn't making a clean electrical connection to the bit.
FIX: Clean both contact surfaces before each probe session; confirm the probe clip has a solid connection point on the collet/bit, not paint or coating.

--- Entry 145 ---
KEY: Machine loses steps only on fast rapid moves
MEANS: Acceleration setting is too aggressive for the motor/driver/mechanical combination at that speed.
FIX: Lower the max acceleration setting for that axis in firmware config until rapids are reliable, then dial back up carefully.

--- Entry 146 ---
KEY: USB connection is fine but jogging feels laggy/delayed
MEANS: Too many status-poll requests queued, or a very low system resource situation on the host PC.
FIX: Close unnecessary background apps; if using a Raspberry Pi or similar low-power host, confirm it isn't thermal-throttling.

--- Entry 147 ---
KEY: Machine position drifts over a long job (ends up slightly off from where it started)
MEANS: Missed steps accumulating from marginal current/acceleration settings, or backlash in the mechanical drivetrain.
FIX: Check for backlash by jogging one direction then reversing while watching for dead travel; verify current settings are adequate for the load.


######################################################################
# J. Standard GRBL real-time status report fields
######################################################################

--- Entry 148 ---
KEY: Idle
MEANS: Machine is powered, homed or not, and not moving. Ready to accept commands.
FIX: Normal resting state — no fix needed.

--- Entry 149 ---
KEY: Run
MEANS: Machine is actively executing a motion command.
FIX: Normal — no fix needed.

--- Entry 150 ---
KEY: Hold:0
MEANS: Feed hold is active and deceleration has completed; motion is fully stopped, ready to resume.
FIX: Send cycle-start (~) or press Resume in the app to continue.

--- Entry 151 ---
KEY: Hold:1
MEANS: Feed hold was requested but the machine is still decelerating to a stop.
FIX: Wait a moment for it to settle into Hold:0 before resuming.

--- Entry 152 ---
KEY: Jog
MEANS: Machine is executing a jog command.
FIX: Normal during manual jogging — no fix needed.

--- Entry 153 ---
KEY: Alarm
MEANS: Machine is in an alarm-locked state and will reject motion commands until cleared.
FIX: Send $X (unlock) or use the app's Clear Alarm button; investigate the specific ALARM:N code first.

--- Entry 154 ---
KEY: Door:0
MEANS: Safety door is closed and the door-hold has been cleared.
FIX: Normal — no fix needed.

--- Entry 155 ---
KEY: Door:1
MEANS: Safety door is currently open; machine is holding.
FIX: Close the door/interlock switch to allow resume.

--- Entry 156 ---
KEY: Door:2
MEANS: Door is closed but the machine is still resuming after being held.
FIX: Wait for it to settle to Door:0/Idle.

--- Entry 157 ---
KEY: Door:3
MEANS: Door was opened during a hold that was already in progress from another cause.
FIX: Close the door, then clear/resume as needed.

--- Entry 158 ---
KEY: Check
MEANS: Machine is in G-code check mode — parsing without moving, for validating a file before a real run.
FIX: Send $C again to exit check mode when done reviewing.

--- Entry 159 ---
KEY: Home
MEANS: Machine is actively executing a homing cycle.
FIX: Normal during $H — no fix needed.

--- Entry 160 ---
KEY: Sleep
MEANS: Machine has entered a low-power sleep state after $SLP or an idle timeout.
FIX: Send any command or reset to wake it.

--- Entry 161 ---
KEY: MPos:
MEANS: Machine position — absolute coordinates relative to the homed origin, ignoring any work offset.
FIX: Reference field, not an error — use to sanity-check the machine thinks it's where you expect.

--- Entry 162 ---
KEY: WPos:
MEANS: Work position — position relative to the currently active work coordinate system (G54 etc.).
FIX: Reference field — if this looks wildly wrong, check your WCS offset, not the machine itself.

--- Entry 163 ---
KEY: WCO:
MEANS: Work coordinate offset currently applied — the difference between MPos and WPos.
FIX: A WCO more than a few tens of mm from expected is the same signature ECSS Module 2's WCS-health check flags.

--- Entry 164 ---
KEY: Bf:
MEANS: Planner and RX buffer availability (blocks free, bytes free) — flow-control info for the sender.
FIX: Consistently near-zero available buffer suggests the sender is pushing faster than the board can plan — reduce feed or check for a stall.

--- Entry 165 ---
KEY: FS:
MEANS: Current feed rate and spindle speed actually being executed.
FIX: Compare to what you commanded — a mismatch can indicate an override slider is active.

--- Entry 166 ---
KEY: Pn:
MEANS: Currently active/triggered input pins (limit switches, probe, door, e-stop) reported by letter code.
FIX: If a letter appears here with nothing physically triggering it, suspect a wiring/EMI/pull-up issue on that input.

--- Entry 167 ---
KEY: Ov:
MEANS: Active override percentages for feed, rapid, and spindle.
FIX: Check this if motion seems faster/slower than commanded — an override slider may be left away from 100%.

--- Entry 168 ---
KEY: A:
MEANS: Active accessory state flags — spindle direction, flood/mist coolant.
FIX: Reference field — cross-check against what you expect from your M3/M7/M8 commands.

--- Entry 169 ---
KEY: Ln:
MEANS: Current line number being executed, when line numbers are enabled in the status report mask.
FIX: Useful for locating exactly where in the file a stall or unexpected stop happened.


######################################################################
# K. Common GRBL $-setting misconfigurations (generic/GRBL-fallback path)
######################################################################

--- Entry 170 ---
KEY: $0 (step pulse time)
MEANS: Too short a value for your driver can cause missed steps that look like random position drift.
FIX: Increase toward 10us if you see unexplained drift with no other cause.

--- Entry 171 ---
KEY: $1 (step idle delay)
MEANS: Controls how long motors stay energized after a move; set wrong, axes can drift/sag when idle on a low-friction axis (like Z).
FIX: Set to 255 (always on) if an axis sags when idle, especially Z with a heavy spindle.

--- Entry 172 ---
KEY: $2 / $3 (step/direction port invert masks)
MEANS: Wrong bits here make an axis run backwards or double-step.
FIX: Toggle the specific axis's bit rather than rewiring the motor.

--- Entry 173 ---
KEY: $5 (limit pin invert)
MEANS: Wrong polarity here makes the board think a switch is always triggered (instant alarm on boot) or never triggered (homing timeout).
FIX: Match this to whether your switches are normally-open or normally-closed.

--- Entry 174 ---
KEY: $6 (probe pin invert)
MEANS: Wrong polarity here is the single most common cause of 'probe did not contact' or a probe that triggers immediately.
FIX: Flip this bit if a probe cycle either times out every time or fails instantly.

--- Entry 175 ---
KEY: $10 (status report mask)
MEANS: Controls which fields (WPos vs MPos, buffer state, etc.) appear in the status line the app parses.
FIX: If the app shows blank/missing position fields, confirm this mask includes what the host expects.

--- Entry 176 ---
KEY: $20 (soft limits enable)
MEANS: Requires homing enabled first; if set without $22=1 it will error (error:10) or simply not take effect.
FIX: Enable $22 before $20.

--- Entry 177 ---
KEY: $21 (hard limits enable)
MEANS: If enabled with noisy/unshielded switch wiring, causes random false-trip alarms.
FIX: Disable temporarily to confirm noise is the cause, then fix wiring/shielding rather than leaving it off long-term.

--- Entry 178 ---
KEY: $22 (homing cycle enable)
MEANS: Must be on for $H / the app's Home button to do anything.
FIX: Enable if Home silently does nothing.

--- Entry 179 ---
KEY: $23 (homing direction invert mask)
MEANS: Wrong bits send an axis homing away from its switch instead of toward it.
FIX: Flip the bit for any axis that moves the wrong way when you press Home.

--- Entry 180 ---
KEY: $24 / $25 (homing feed / seek rate)
MEANS: Too fast a seek rate can make the board overshoot and miss detecting the switch cleanly.
FIX: Lower the seek rate if homing is inconsistent run-to-run.

--- Entry 181 ---
KEY: $27 (homing pull-off distance)
MEANS: Too small a pull-off can leave the switch still pressed after backing off, causing ALARM:8.
FIX: Increase pull-off distance a small amount if you see repeated pull-off failures.

--- Entry 182 ---
KEY: $30 / $31 (max/min spindle speed)
MEANS: If your configured RPM range doesn't match the real spindle/VFD, PWM-to-RPM mapping will be wrong.
FIX: Set these to match the actual spindle's real min/max RPM.

--- Entry 183 ---
KEY: $32 (laser mode)
MEANS: If enabled on a spindle (not laser) machine, motion dynamics change (no G0 spindle-off behavior) unexpectedly.
FIX: Only enable for an actual laser head.

--- Entry 184 ---
KEY: $100-$102 (X/Y/Z steps/mm)
MEANS: Wrong values here are the most common cause of parts coming out the wrong physical size.
FIX: Recalibrate by commanding a known move distance and measuring the real travel, then correct proportionally.

--- Entry 185 ---
KEY: $110-$112 (X/Y/Z max rate)
MEANS: Set higher than the motor/driver/mechanics can actually achieve, causes stalls and lost steps at high feed.
FIX: Lower until rapids are reliable, then verify with a squareness/repeatability test.

--- Entry 186 ---
KEY: $120-$122 (X/Y/Z acceleration)
MEANS: Too aggressive causes lost steps on direction changes and corners; too conservative wastes cycle time.
FIX: Tune down if you see steps lost specifically at sharp corners or direction reversals.

--- Entry 187 ---
KEY: $130-$132 (X/Y/Z max travel)
MEANS: Used by soft limits AND by ECSS Module 1's pre-flight envelope check — a value of 0 here causes false-positive blocks on every job.
FIX: Set these to your machine's real physical travel in mm, not 0 and not left default.

--- Entry 200 ---

NAV: Settings > Integrations
